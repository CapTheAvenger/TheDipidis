Get Started
Orientation

Six categories. One landing page.

The Meta Hub is the homepage. Pick a data source and drill in. Every other tool links back here, so it's the natural starting point for any session.

  • City League Meta — live share from Japan's weekly cardshop tournaments. Tier shifts week-over-week, the earliest leading indicator before international Majors.
  • Deck Analysis (Japan) — deep-dive into individual JP decks: card distributions, common stock lists, tech picks, matchup WR from Japanese tournaments.
  • Current Meta (Global) — online tournament data from play.limitlesstcg.com. Top-8 / Top-16 conversion per deck. Closer to the EU / US competitive scene.
  • Deck Analysis (Global) — deep-dive into globally played online decks. Card lists, opening-hand stats, win rates for players competing internationally.
  • Past Meta — historical Major data (Regionals, Internationals, Worlds) plus results from previous Standard formats. Learn from history to sharpen future meta calls.
  • Meta Call — predict the meta share for your next tournament, blend the online data with your own estimates, get expected encounters per round with a matchup overview.
  • Card Legend on the homepage — directly below the six tiles, a sample card explains every badge, button, and stat line you'll meet on real cards across the app: Max-Count, In Deck, Wishlist, owned-other-prints, set + inclusion rate, Ø-counts, action buttons, plus Pin / Exclude for Cooking Mode. One legend, every screen.
Pro tip: The Card Legend is the fastest way to onboard yourself — scan it once and every badge across the deck-analysis, builder, and binder pages becomes self-explanatory.

Japan's Weekly Pulse

City League Meta — who's winning, who's losing, week over week.

Pick City League Meta from the homepage and you land on Japan's full cardshop-circuit snapshot — the largest competitive ecosystem in the world, ~2 300 decks deep, refreshed daily. The page is built around a single question: which deck is winning the format right now, and which way is it moving?

  • Headline · 2 298 deck entries — the badge at the top of the page is the sample-size anchor. Every percentage below is computed against this count, so you always know whether you're reading a 7-day burst (small N) or a stabilised 30-day window (large N). Click it to widen the data window.
  • Tier brackets with archetype art — every archetype gets its own card with the actual key-Pokémon TCG art as background and two stat-badges underneath. The orange Rank N badge is the deck's position in the format ranking; the neutral Share X % badge is its slice of total deck entries. Each badge carries an inline ▲ / ▼ / — trend chip with the absolute pp / rank change vs the prior identical-length window. Dragapult ex · Rank 1 ▲ 2 · Share 14,2 % ▲ 0,4 reads as "moved up 2 ranks and gained 0,4 pp share since last week".
  • Tier 1 / 2 / 3 cutoffs are computed, not fixed — Tier 1 Meta Dominators (red), Tier 2 Strong Contenders (orange), Tier 3 Niche Picks (green). Decks land in a tier based on a rolling composite score: brought share + Top-8 conversion + 30-day WR, not on a hard share threshold. A Tier 3 deck with high Top-8 conversion is your tech-target signal.
  • Variant counter ("26 var.") — the count next to each tier-header tells you how many sub-variants the database recognises for that archetype-family. Dragapult ex · 26 variants means Dragapult-ex, Dragapult Dusknoir, Dragapult Meowth, Dragapult Froslass + 22 more all roll up into that one card. The Full Comparison table below splits them apart if you need per-variant numbers.
  • Click any tier card → card-level Deck Analysis — the entire tier card is a click target. One tap takes you to the archetype's per-card breakdown (Main Cards / Tech / Counter / Rare Picks). Same target also reachable from any of the other tables below.
  • Performance Improvers + Decliners panel (further down the page) — two ranked mini-tables, green "📈 Improvers" + red "📉 Decliners". Each lists the top-5 archetypes whose share % moved by ≥ 0,4 pp vs the prior window, sorted by absolute Δ. Surfaces "Slowking just jumped +2,3 pp" without scrolling the full comparison table. Decks with insufficient movement are hidden so the panel always shows clean signal.
  • Overall vs Top-8 Conversion panel — ranks every deck two ways at once: Brought Share (raw deck count ÷ total entries) on the left, Top-8 Conversion (Top-8 finishes ÷ same deck's entries) on the right. The "low share, high punch" decks — high Top-8 rank, low Brought-Share rank — are exactly the ones you want to tech against even if they don't dominate the headline. Hydrapple Ogerpon at #1 in Top-8 but outside Top-3 in Brought Share is the canonical example.
  • Past Meta toggle — dropdown at the top swaps the active feed from "Current snapshot" (auto-updating) to a frozen earlier format window (TEF-POR, SVI-ASC, SVI-PFL, …). Same screens, same data shape, different time window — useful for "what was the meta when Charizard ex rotated in?" reads.
Pro tip: The "Season pause" banner appears between City League seasons when no new events are running — the historical filters and deck-analysis tools below still work against the last available snapshot, so the page never goes dark.

Drill Into Any Deck

Click a deck name. See exactly how players build it.

Tap any archetype in the Top Archetypes grid, the Tier brackets, or the Full Comparison Table — and you jump straight into the card-level Deck Analysis page. The full breakdown of how the deck actually gets put together.

  • Subdivided by section — Main Cards (staples + #1 Ace Spec), Tech / Counter cards, and a "Rare Picks (click to collapse)" block for edge cases. Each section auto-folds so you scan the shape, not a 60-card wall.
  • Per-card stats — share % within the archetype (100 % on Hydreigon ex, 95 % on Lillie's Determination), average copies per deck, exact set+number, rarity tier, and live Cardmarket € price.
  • Date filter — narrow to a specific tournament window (e.g. only the last 2 weeks of a rotation, or a single shop's events) and every stat re-computes.
  • Share-% filter — show only cards in ≥ 90 % of decks (the core staples), ≥ 70 % (consensus picks), or ≥ 50 % (the full body). Cuts noise instantly.
  • Current ↔ Past Meta toggle — swap the period at the top without leaving the page. Same deck, different snapshot, instant comparison.
  • ★ Show all artworks — tap the yellow star on any card to pop up every known print and swap one in with a single click. Perfect for finding a cheaper print of an expensive staple — or upgrading a build to Full Art for the binder.
Pro tip: The "Rare Picks" section is gold for tech-hunting. It surfaces cards that only appear in 5–20 % of lists but are the difference between a placement and a top cut — Ethan's Sudowoodo, Lillie's Determination, Genesect, that kind of thing.

From Analysis to Build

Deck Builder — generate, tweak, share.

Same Card Overview, with the Generate panel that turns the per-card averages into a tournament-accurate 60-card list. Eight buttons cover everything from "build it" to "ship it to a Discord chat".

  • Consistency — auto-generates the 60-card list from per-card avg-when-used. Math.round + Largest-Remainder + Reverse-LRM math; lands exactly on 60.
  • Max Rarity ↔ Low Rarity — flip every card to its highest- or lowest-rarity print in one click. Bidirectional toggle. Resets to Max on every fresh Generate.
  • Test Draw — opening-hand simulator. Draws 7 cards + tracks mulligans (Basic in hand 77.8 % · Mulligan 22.2 % in the screenshot).
  • TCG Showdown ↗ — copies the list and opens tcg-showdown.com in a new tab for full external playtesting.
  • Save — persists the build to your profile's My Decks with a name + format tag. Synced across devices.
  • Compare — side-by-side diff with another build (paste a list or pick a saved one). Colour-coded changes.
  • Deck → Proxy — sends the whole 60 to the Proxy Printer queue with one click. Sleeve and test.
  • Share — exports the built deck as a full-color PNG you can paste straight into Discord / Telegram / WhatsApp. No clipping, no link previews.
Pro tip: The Search bar above the card grid accepts name (EN or DE) or set + number — handy when you want to find a specific tech card without scrolling through the full list. Tap the result and it slots straight into the build.
Current International Meta · Analysis + Cooking + Tech Lab

The deepest tab in TheDipidis. Same screens as City League — much more data, many more knobs.

The hub view

The whole format on one screen — sample size always visible.

Brought Share, Top-8 Conversion and the Archetype Overview live side by side in the same tab. The deck-entry counter at the top makes it obvious whether you're reading a 7-day burst or a stabilised 30-day window.

  • Brought Share % — (decks of an archetype) ÷ (total deck entries in the active window). Variants are listed separately so a Dragapult-ex line and a Dragapult Dusknoir line don't get conflated.
  • Top-8 Conversion — (Top-8 finishes) ÷ (deck entries of the same archetype). Surfaces the "low share, high punch" decks that brought show alone hides.
  • Archetype Overview — every legal archetype in the current format with a one-glance legality stamp (e.g. TEF-CRI) and the rolling climbers/fallers with WR delta vs the previous window.
  • Live entry counter — the 6 687 deck entries badge re-prints with every refresh; small samples flag themselves visually instead of hiding behind a confident-looking percentage.
Pro tip: If a row sits at < 30 games in the window, treat its WR and matchup numbers as directional, not predictive. The Cooking-mode filter row lets you widen the window when the sample is thin.
How "share %" is calculated

Share is always a ratio against the active data window, not against an all-time pool. Default window = last 30 days. Cooking lets you slice that down to a single tournament or stretch it as far back as the rotation goes. The window drives every percentage on the page — change it, and every number recalculates in lock-step.

Tiers + Momentum

Three tiers, weighted by share + conversion + rolling WR.

The tier brackets are a composite — not a pure popularity contest. Share alone would over-promote netdecks; conversion alone would over-promote low-sample regional one-shots. The blend keeps both honest.

  • Tier score — weighted blend of brought share, Top-8 conversion and 30-day WR; updated on every data refresh.
  • ▲ / ▼ arrows — week-over-week tier-score Δ vs the previous identical-length window. Green = rising, red = falling, grey = unchanged.
  • "Strong Contenders" = Tier 2 — high share but conversion just below dominator threshold. These are usually the techs-needed-on-the-fly decks.
  • "Niche Picks" = Tier 3 — low share, high conversion. Best Tech Lab fodder; small targets that punch up.
Pro tip: A deck moving from Tier 2 → Tier 1 with a green arrow + climbing top-8 conversion is the cleanest "this is the real winner" signal you can ask for.

Format Staples

The cards 9 in 10 lists share — and the few that move the meta.

Format Staples ranks every card by format-wide inclusion: the percentage of all active deck entries that run at least one copy. Night Stretcher at 100 %, Lillie's at 97.1 %, and so on. This is the price of admission — if a card here drops below 80 % the format is shifting around it.

  • Inclusion % = (decks running ≥ 1 copy) ÷ (total deck entries in window). Card-level, not archetype-level.
  • Tap any row → jumps to the Card Database entry with live Cardmarket price + every print + a wishlist hook.
  • WR Δ on Climbers/Fallers — rolling 14-day matchup-win-rate change. A faller archetype with no card-pool change is usually getting teched against, not weakened structurally.
Pro tip: Cross-reference a falling archetype's drop with the Most-Used Cards list. If a staple in that archetype's typical engine slipped, you can usually point at the exact card that's failing the meta speed-check.

How the Meta Call field is composed

Field % per opponent = weighted blend of the last 5 in-person Majors + the rolling 14-day online ladder. Recent in-person results get an underdog-champion booster (a regional winner crossing < 2 % usage gets bumped one week ahead of the model). Format-leader consolidation kicks in within multi-variant families — once a parent archetype crosses 25 % within-family share, sub-variants get folded back into the parent's bucket so the field isn't double-counted.

Build vs… — flow part 1

Pick your targets. Pick how aggressive. Move on.

The Build-vs… modal asks two questions: against whom, and how much of your consistency are you willing to spend on tech. Everything after that is auto-derived.

  • Quick Picks list — the predicted Meta Call field, sorted by share %. Each row shows the field share and a WR pill — red = you're losing this matchup, tech priority; green = you're already favoured, skip.

Build vs… — flow part 2

The engine surfaces candidates — and tells you the WR Δ if you actually build with them.

Suggested Tech Cards are candidates, not commandments. Each card carries its threat tag (RETREAT_LOCK, BENCH_DAMAGE, ABILITY_EX_IMMUNITY, ATTACK_IGNORES_EFFECTS) so you understand exactly why it surfaced. You check the boxes you want and Build.

  • Where suggestions come from — the card-text capability engine scans every card's printed text and tags its interactions. Build vs… then intersects those tags with the threats your picked targets actually pose.
  • Threat tags on each card — labelled in blue next to each suggestion so the "why this card?" is right there. Surfer tagged RETREAT_LOCK against N's Zoroark; Shaymin tagged BENCH_DAMAGE.
  • Lock-in checkboxes — tick the cards you want forced into the next Generate. The button at the bottom updates to "Build with N cards" so you always know what you're committing.

  • Per-opponent breakdown — the table at the bottom shows where the Δ actually came from. You see the +3 pts vs N's Zoroark and Dragapult Dusknoir come from the retreat-lock techs.
  • Estimator cap — Δ is hard-capped at +9 pts to keep claims honest; the per-card threat-bonus is +3 pts per matched threat category.
Pro tip: The detection log near the top ("retreat_lock, surfer, switch") is the audit trail for the Δ. If a Δ feels too good, scan the log — sometimes a threat got double-counted and you can manually un-check one tech to recompute.

Tech Lab

Capability tags on every card. HIGH / MED / LOW match. + Add missing.

Tech Lab walks the card-text capability graph. Pick a target, and the page surfaces the cards that beat it (with the exact text mechanic that does the beating), plus the cards it beats — and a parallel list of Non-EX Attackers when the target's ability blocks only Pokémon ex.

  • Beaten by — cards that defeat this — Card-Text Bypassers (e.g. ABILITY_EX_IMMUNITY attackers that bypass the target's ex-only protection) and Non-EX Attackers when the ability only blocks ex.
  • Good against — cards this one defeats — Specific Meta Matchups (Farigiraf ex, Pikachu ex, Sylveon) with HIGH / MED / LOW match strength from the capability-tag overlap.
  • Capability tags — ATTACK_IGNORES_EFFECTS, ABILITY_EX_IMMUNITY, RETREAT_LOCK, BENCH_DAMAGE and more. Each one comes from a deterministic scan of the printed card text, not a community vote.
  • + Add missing tech — if you spot a card the engine missed, the button registers it as a local override. Stored in localStorage under techLab.overrides.v1 — never leaves your browser.
  • × Not a tech — same idea, opposite direction. Hide a candidate the engine surfaced if your testing confirms it doesn't actually help.
Pro tip: Local overrides are exactly that — local. Community rollout is planned for a follow-up release: aggregate confirmed overrides → ship them in the next data file → the engine learns from everybody. The more people use Tech Lab, the better the tech suggestions get for the whole community.
The data file behind Tech Lab

Suggestions come from data/card_capability_*.json — a deterministic scan of every printed card's ability + attack text into a set of capability tags. The page reads those files at boot, applies your local overrides on top, then ranks suggestions for the picked target by tag-overlap strength.

The roadmap: community overrides shipped in a follow-up release after they're aggregated and reviewed.

When NOT to trust the Cooking suggestions

If the matchup matrix shows fewer than 30 logged games, treat the WR as directional. If your target's archetype was released within the past 7 days, the tag scan may not have caught all its interactions yet — open Tech Lab and use "+ Add missing tech" to fill the gap. If two of your three Build-vs… targets fall in the same threat category, expect Δ to over-report.

The Cooking Promise: the engine flags candidates; you still pick the build.
