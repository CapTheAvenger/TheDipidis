Getting started

The Dipidis in five minutes

The Dipidis shows which decks are being played in the Pokémon Trading Card Game right now and how well they do — from real tournament results. You find a deck, take over the list, save it and keep track of how your games go.

1 Your own deck in five steps

  1. Look at the meta. The start page "Meta" shows which archetypes are played how often and how often they win. At the top you pick the data space: Global, Japan (City League) or Past formats.
  2. Pick a deck. Under "Deck analysis" you choose an archetype. You see which cards the tournament lists play and how many copies are usual.
  3. Take over the list. "Save as my deck" right below the archetype picker builds the typical list and saves it to your account. Without an account you can look at everything but not save.
  4. Use the deck. Under "My Decks" you copy the list for Pokémon TCG Live, print proxies or compare decks. "+ New deck" opens "Build a deck" for a list of your own.
  5. Record your games. In the Battle Journal you enter every game: opposing deck, result, who went first. That shows which decks your list does well or badly against.

2 Where is what?

The tab bar sits at the bottom (phone) or top:

  • Meta — the start page: shares, win rates, tiers, matchups.
  • Deck analysis — one archetype in detail: cards, copy counts, tournament lists.
  • Meta Call — a forecast of which decks to expect at your next tournament and how your chance at Day 2 looks against them.
  • Champions and Pocket — two other Pokémon games, see the glossary.

Everything else opens from ☰ Menu next to the logo: profile with My Decks, wishlist, collection and Battle Journal, "Build a deck", card database, Past formats, Japan (City League), proxy printer, calculator, this page and "Sources & Method" — which says where every number comes from.

Where the numbers come from. From reported tournament results (Limitless Online, Limitless Labs, Limitless Japan), prices from Cardmarket. If something is missing for a deck or format, it says "no data" instead of an estimated number. The details are under "Sources & Method".

3 Glossary

The terms on the site, briefly explained. An ⓘ next to a figure leads straight here.

Meta
Which decks are being played right now and how well they do. "The meta" is the set of decks you will face in the current format.
Archetype
A kind of deck, named after its key Pokémon (e.g. "Dragapult"). All lists with the same core count as the same archetype, even if single cards differ.
Variant
A version of an archetype with a different partner, e.g. "Dragapult" and "Dragapult Dusknoir". On the start page variants sit under their family.
Format (e.g. TEF–30C)
Which cards are allowed in tournaments. The name gives the oldest and the newest legal set: TEF–30C means "from Temporal Forces to the newest set". It is not a set code — the card search finds nothing with it.
Rotation · Past formats
Once a year the oldest sets leave the Standard format ("rotation"). "Past formats" holds the tournament data of finished formats, frozen at their final state.
Standard · Expanded
Standard is the tournament format with the newest sets (see Format). Expanded allows much older cards. The site analyses Standard; in the archetype box the filter "Expanded only" shows the cards no longer allowed in Standard.
Online · Major
Online: reported online tournaments on Limitless. Major: in-person events from regional size upwards. Both often stand side by side, because decks do differently online and in person.
Share
How often a deck was played, measured against all entries of the source. Online, it is the share of all Limitless online entries since the format began.
Win rate (WR)
How often a deck wins. On the deck tiles and in the heatmap: wins divided by decided matches, W/(W+L) — ties are left out so online and major compare. Other places use other formulas (ties in the denominator, or match points); every rate names its formula in the hint next to it.
Matchup (MU) · Heatmap
How a deck does against one specific other deck. The heatmap shows this for all common pairings: per cell the win rate online and major, below it "M" = number of matches. Italic means fewer than 10 matches, so uncertain.
Top 8 · Top-8 rate
The best eight of a tournament. The top-8 rate says how often a deck got there from its entries.
Day 2 · Day-2 rate
Large in-person events (Regionals, Internationals) run over two days; only players with enough wins on day one play on day two. The Day-2 rate says how often a deck makes it.
Tier · Rogue
Tiers group decks by share and success (Tier 1 = the strongest and most played). Rogue decks are rare decks outside the tiers — often with few results, so with small numbers they show the record instead of a rate.
Record (W–L–T)
Wins – losses – ties, always in this order.
Tournament types
League Challenge and League Cup: small local events. Regional: large in-person event with Day 2. Special Event: large events outside the Regionals. International: the continental championships (e.g. EUIC, NAIC). Worlds: the World Championships.
City League
The tournament series in Japan. Japan gets new sets earlier, so the City League shows which decks may soon matter here too.
ACE SPEC
Especially strong cards; a deck may contain only one of them in total.
Tech
A single card a list adds on purpose against specific opponents.
Proxy · Original · Missing
A proxy is a printed stand-in for a card you do not own (yet) — for testing, not for tournaments. In the archetype box "Original", "Proxy" and "Missing" show how you own each card right now.
PTCGL
Pokémon TCG Live, the official online game. "PTCGL" buttons copy a list in the text format you can paste there directly.
IRL
"In real life": the deck exists as real cards on your table.
Set code
The short code of an expansion, e.g. TWM for Twilight Masquerade. A card is identified by set code and card number, not by its name.
BO1 · BO3 · Mulligan · Brick · No-show
BO1/BO3: one game or up to three games per match. Mulligan: no Basic Pokémon in the opening hand, shuffle again. Brick: a hand you cannot do anything useful with. No-show: the opponent did not turn up.
Meta Call
The forecast for an upcoming tournament: which decks to expect how often and how your deck gets to Day 2 against them. You can adjust the tournament type and your own estimates.
Archetype box
A card list per archetype for your real collection: which cards belong in the box, which you have and which are missing. Created via Past formats → card overview → "Add to archetype box".
Masterclass
Bought deck guides, prepared in German. They live in the profile under "My Masterclasses".
Champions · Pocket
Champions is a Pokémon video game (not a card game) with double battles; the tab shows which Pokémon are played there and helps with team building. Pocket is Pokémon TCG Pocket, a separate mobile game with its own cards and rules. Both are separate from the trading card game.
Consistency
Consistency: how reliably a deck plays its turns. "Max Consistency" in the deck builder builds a list from an archetype's most-played cards.
Binder
A collector's binder as a list. The Meta Binder holds every card of the current meta and shows what you are missing; you build the Custom Binder from your own decks. Missing cards go to the wishlist or the Proxy Printer.
Play box
In the archetype boxes: every Standard-legal card from your boxes at its highest played count (at most 4). It updates itself — new cards are added, rotated ones drop out. You tick off what you own there.
Regulation Mark
The letter at the bottom left of a card. It decides whether the card is legal in Standard; currently the letters H, I and J. Basic energy is always legal.
Side Quest · Replica
Side Quest holds the other Pokémon games (Champions, Pocket), separate from the card game. A Replica code is a team code from Pokémon Champions: tap to copy it, paste it in the game to load the team.
